Shared core: three clocks

Proposed target

Both blueprints use the same three timing targets. A live event and an ML score refresh are separate operations. The demo shows and measures both.

60 s

From a live event to its appearance in the Customer 360 view.

15 min

Between refreshes of features, segments and churn scores.

5 min

From a successful scoring run to the retention alert.

Do not claim more than the evidence shows

“90 days early”

Calculate the score 90 days before renewal. Freeze the information available at that checkpoint. Evaluate the later renewal outcome. Predicting churn "within 90 days" does not establish 90 days of warning.

“We know why”

Feature attributions explain how model inputs affect the score. Checked events support investigation. Neither proves the customer's true motivation. Neither proves that a retention action will work.

“We saved revenue”

A demo can show recurring revenue at risk and recorded actions. Proving improved retention requires evidence from real retention actions and a comparison group. Synthetic results prove only that the process works.

Show the proof

What the demo and video must show, whichever scenario we choose.

  • Test on a later time period that training excludes. Do not use information from after each feature snapshot.
  • Compare the model with a simple rules baseline.
  • Measure precision: the share of flagged customers that churn. Measure recall: the share of churned customers that the model flags.
  • Evaluate precision and recall at the number of customers the team can contact.
  • Measure the time between the warning and the churn outcome.
  • Show one live event that creates exactly one retention case. Keep a record that supports an audit.
  • Show a healthy or seasonal customer, not only the scripted failure.
  • Demonstrate ML segments and alert-routing rules separately. Do not label rules as ML.

How one warning becomes one action

Company, customer and counterexample (illustrative)

The company and customer

HomeBridge is a fictional mortgage lender. It services repayment mortgages and offers product transfers when fixed-rate deals end.

The customer unit is one mortgage account. Joint borrowers remain linked parties, not duplicate customers. A servicing adviser owns each review.

The problem and counterexample

Identify external refinancing risk 90 days before a fixed-rate deal ends. Route payment difficulties to support before any commercial retention action.

Alex reports concern about future payments while a product-transfer application repeatedly fails. The adviser must inspect both problems.

Sam completes a product transfer without payment problems. A quiet portal history alone does not justify an alert.

01 / Submission summary

Title: HomeBridge Renewal and Payment-Support Desk.

Our proposed Fabric solution combines mortgage, payment, application, and servicing history. It predicts external refinancing and explains evidence for human review.

A separate support policy takes priority when payment difficulties appear. The prototype does not approve lending, change rates, or recommend financial products.

Distinctive value: distinguish commercial loss from payment distress. A retained mortgage is not automatically a good customer outcome.

Scope: fixed-rate repayment accounts, one renewal cohort, one application-failure replay, and one support case. Default prediction is a separate future extension.

The account view shows deal end, current terms, application problems, segment, score age, evidence, owner, and support priority.

Use synthetic histories with complete outcomes, reversals, delayed feeds, and successful transfers. Generate labels from separate latent factors, not the alert rule.

An indicative future payment requires dated rate assumptions and a checked calculation. Label it as an illustration, not an offer or affordability decision.

03 / Proposed architecture and interfaces
  1. Load dated account, payment, application, and servicing exports through Fabric pipelines into raw Lakehouse tables.
  2. Validate keys and ledger history. Create clean account histories and frozen feature snapshots in notebooks.
  3. Train renewal models and behavioural clusters. Register feature signatures, experiments, and model versions with MLflow.
  4. Publish scores, segments, explanations, and evidence references to curated Gold Delta tables. A Power BI semantic model reads them in Direct Lake mode for a restricted account view.
  5. Replay an application failure through Eventstream into Eventhouse. Retain original event IDs and timestamps.
  6. Run KQL journey queries in Eventhouse. One query lists each application's stages and errors in time order. One measures days stalled at a stage. One counts retries after a platform error.
  7. Run an explicit scoring notebook for the replay. A Fabric pipeline also runs scoring and segment assignment on a schedule. Publish risk_updated only after scoring succeeds.
  8. Use Activator and Power Automate to create a human-owned review case in a secured SharePoint list.

Contract: risk_updated contains event_id, mortgage_id, cycle_id, snapshot_id, model_version, scored_at, target, score, and evidence IDs.

Read current support eligibility at case creation. Support priority does not depend on a high churn score or account value.

Enforce a unique case_key of mortgage_id + cycle_id + action_family. Duplicate writes read the existing case. Other failures remain visible.

04 / Prediction and segmentation

Checkpoint: deal_end_at minus 90 days. Predict confirmed external refinancing between the checkpoint and deal_end_at plus 30 days.

Internal transfers and continued servicing are negative outcomes only after the full window. Sales, bereavement closures, and unidentified redemptions remain outside this target.

Use known redemption reasons or verified refinance records. Incomplete observation produces an unknown label, not assumed retention.

Compare logistic regression and a small tree model with an application-friction rules baseline. Use application completion, platform errors, complaint age, and contact history.

Derive at least three behavioural signals from dated history: failed application attempts in the last 30 days against the last 90 days, days stalled at one application stage, and the trend in servicing contacts.

Exclude sensitive support details, protected characteristics, names, and fine-grained location from the first prediction model. Check remaining features for unjustified proxies.

Fit two to five behavioural clusters on application and servicing features. Inspect stability and centroids before naming groups.

Fit clusters once per model release. Each scheduled run assigns accounts to the fitted clusters, so a segment ID keeps its meaning over time. Each assignment is a new row in segment_membership.

Test later deal cohorts with borrower groups outside training. Require both event and ingestion timestamps at or before the checkpoint.

Training labels must exist before the simulated model release. Freeze thresholds on validation data. Report accuracy, precision, recall, calibration, and lift at ten reviews per run.

Lift compares the refinancing rate in the ten highest-scored accounts with the cohort's base rate. Accuracy alone misleads when few accounts leave.

Store per-account feature attributions with each score: SHAP values for the tree model and coefficient contributions for logistic regression. They explain the score, not the borrower's motive.

Payment difficulty uses an explicit support policy, not a hidden churn label. Predicting default within 90 days would not establish 90 days of advance warning.

05 / Alert policy and failure handling

Route an active payment-support request to trained servicing staff first. Suppress commercial retention tasks while the support case requires review.

Without a support priority, route verified application failures to technical support and eligible renewal reviews to a servicing adviser.

A human checks contact policy and suitability before any customer action. The prototype sends no messages and changes no mortgage terms.

Record model version, snapshot, evidence IDs, routing policy, owner, decision, and ingestion, scoring, alert, and case timestamps.

Quarantine unknown accounts and conflicting borrower mappings. Flag stale payment feeds. Retry case failures without duplicate cases.

A payment reversal requests review and rescoring. It does not erase the audit or automatically close a support case.
