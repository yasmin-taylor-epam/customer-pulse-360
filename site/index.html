01 / WorkHarbour / Field-service software Maintenance companies use WorkHarbour to schedule jobs and create invoices under annual contracts. Invoice failures and reopened support tickets could put renewal at risk. Explore the company, evidence, and proposed solution

The company

WorkHarbour sells cloud software to maintenance companies. Dispatchers schedule engineers. Engineers record completed jobs. Office staff create invoices.

Customers pay per field engineer under annual contracts. The subscribing company is the customer, not each engineer.

A customer-success manager owns adoption and renewal. Fewer licences affect revenue but remain a separate prediction target.

The problem statement

Find companies at risk of not renewing, about 90 days before renewal. Show evidence that supports an approved retention action.

A fictional customer, Alder Maintenance, completes fewer jobs through the software. Invoice failures and reopened tickets accompany that decline.

The manager needs to distinguish workflow failure from seasonal demand before choosing an action.

Assumed existing stack

  • An Azure App Service API serves the dispatcher portal and engineer app.
  • Azure SQL stores accounts, contracts, jobs, and invoice outcomes.
  • Application Insights stores workflow failures and timestamps.
  • A support platform exports ticket severity, resolution times, and reopen events.
  • A CRM stores renewal dates, account owners, and approved contact activity.

Evidence to collect

  • Completed jobs and active licensed seats over time, not logins alone.
  • Invoice failure rates, affected workflows, and linked support tickets.
  • Contract value, renewal date, account tenure, and observed renewal outcome.
  • Seasonal history and missing records that could change the interpretation.

Counterexample: another company uses the product less during its quiet season, without workflow or support problems.

Proposed solution: a renewal investigation desk

  1. Join account, contract, product, and support records in a Fabric Lakehouse using known account IDs.
  2. Create dated features for usage trends, workflow failures, and unresolved support problems.
  3. Train a renewal classifier and behavioural clusters in Fabric notebooks. Record experiments with MLflow.
  4. Show segment, risk drivers, contract value, and source evidence in a Power BI account view.
  5. Replay a support escalation through Eventstream and Eventhouse. Refresh the score through a separate, explicit operation.
  6. Use Activator to flag eligible accounts. Create one retention case for a manager to review.

First action: escalate the invoice incident or restore adoption before renewal. Do not assume the customer intends to switch competitors.
